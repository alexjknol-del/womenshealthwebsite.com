A Practical Guide to Lubricant Safety and Ingredients

Lubricant use is common and safe, but not all formulations are equal from a health standpoint. Clinical research has flagged several ingredient categories worth understanding before choosing a product.

Glycerin and added sugars, found in many mainstream water-based lubricants, can feed yeast overgrowth in susceptible individuals and have been associated with a higher incidence of bacterial vaginosis in some studies. This doesn't mean every glycerin-containing lubricant causes problems, but it's a relevant factor for anyone prone to recurrent infections.

Osmolality — a measure of how a lubricant interacts with cell membranes — is a less well-known but clinically relevant factor. Products with very high osmolality can dehydrate and irritate delicate tissue over repeated use; the World Health Organization has published reference ranges for this.

Water-based lubricants remain the most universally compatible choice, safe with both condoms and silicone products, while silicone-based lubricants last longer but should not be paired with silicone toys.
