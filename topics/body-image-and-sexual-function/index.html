How Body Image Affects Sexual Function — and What Helps

Body image and sexual function are more directly linked than most people assume. Research on "spectatoring" — the tendency to mentally step outside yourself and monitor how you look during intimacy — shows it consistently reduces both arousal and satisfaction.

Sensate focus, a technique developed in sex therapy by Masters and Johnson and still used clinically today, addresses this directly by redirecting attention toward physical sensation rather than appearance during intimacy. It's one of the more evidence-supported interventions in this area.

Context effects are well documented too: lighting, familiarity of setting, and verbal reassurance from a partner all measurably reduce self-monitoring behavior during intimacy, independent of any change in actual appearance.

These techniques tend to work best as an ongoing practice rather than a one-time fix, since the underlying habit of self-monitoring typically took years to form.
