Intimate Hygiene: What the Evidence Actually Supports

Intimate hygiene is one of the more misunderstood areas of women's health, largely because it's marketed as a category that needs constant intervention when the opposite is closer to true. The vagina maintains its own microbiome, dominated by protective Lactobacillus bacteria that keep pH low and unwanted organisms in check.

Clinical guidance is consistent on this point: the vagina does not need to be cleaned internally. External washing with a mild, unscented, pH-balanced product is sufficient, and internal douching is associated with a higher, not lower, rate of bacterial vaginosis and other infections.

Fragranced products — soaps, wipes, sprays — are among the most common irritants reported in gynecology visits for vulvar discomfort. Ingredient sensitivity varies significantly between individuals, so switching to fragrance-free options is a reasonable first step when irritation appears without an obvious cause.

Fabric choice affects moisture retention meaningfully. Breathable, natural fibers like cotton reduce the risk of yeast overgrowth compared to synthetic materials worn for extended periods, particularly during exercise or in hot weather.
